What are batteries in chess? ↗

In chess, a battery is a tactical formation created by aligning two or more long-range pieces—such as queens, rooks, or bishops—on the same rank, file, or diagonal. By combining the firepower of multiple pieces without any intervening obstacles, players can multiply pressure on key target squares, pinned pieces, or the enemy king. Common configurations include queen-and-bishop diagonals, doubled rooks along open files, and Alekhine's gun, which places two rooks ahead of a queen to completely dominate a file. Utilizing batteries allows players to create multi-piece threats that are difficult to defend against, keeping the initiative and accelerating offensive attacks.
