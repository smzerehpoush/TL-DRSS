To eliminate the operational overhead of self-managed Kubernetes control planes, Yelp migrated its Apache Cassandra fleet—comprising nearly 1,000 pods and a petabyte of data across 90 clusters—to Amazon EKS. Instead of using traditional network streaming to replicate data to new nodes, Yelp developed a zero-copy Persistent Volume transplant technique that reattaches existing Amazon EBS volumes directly to new EKS nodes. By pausing Kubernetes operators and sequentially terminating old pods, an automated orchestration script allowed EKS to claim the detached volumes and boot up nodes without customer-facing downtime. This pointer-swap approach moved 1,000 TiB of data, reduced migration duration to roughly 12 hours for their largest cluster, and enabled the complete retirement of legacy infrastructure.
