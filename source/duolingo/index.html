“Y” vs. “e”: What does “y” mean in Spanish? ↗

Spanish uses the word "y" as the default conjunction for "and," but switches to "e" when immediately preceding a word that starts with an "ee" sound. This phonetic rule prevents adjacent identical vowel sounds from blurring together, applying to words starting with "i" or a silent "hi" like "hijo." A similar transformation occurs with the Spanish word for "or," changing from the default "o" to "u" before words starting with an "o" sound. These adjustments help maintain clear word boundaries and natural speech cadence for language learners.
