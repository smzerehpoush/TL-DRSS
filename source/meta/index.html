GEM Training: How Meta Doubled the Efficiency of Its LLM-Scale Ads Foundation Model ↗

Meta's Generative Ads Recommendation Model (GEM) combines trillions of sparse embedding parameters with billions of dense parameters, creating unique training challenges at the intersection of recommendation systems and LLM-scale workloads. To overcome low GPU utilization from variable-length inputs and high communication overhead across thousands of GPUs, Meta implemented hardware/software co-design optimizations targeting both single-GPU and distributed efficiency. The engineering team built custom recommendation kernels like Jagged Flash Attention, deployed an MXFP8 mixed-precision recipe, and designed a topology-aware 5D parallelism strategy with SM-free collectives. These innovations doubled GEM's end-to-end training efficiency to 20–25% Model FLOPs Utilization while scaling training FLOPs 4x over 12 months.

Modernizing the Meta Ads Service With an Open-Source Kernel Scheduler ↗

Meta encountered significant latency regressions in its ad serving fleet following a Linux kernel upgrade, which negatively impacted ad ranking and operational efficiency. To resolve this, they developed a custom scheduling policy using `sched_ext`, an open-source, BPF-based extensible kernel scheduler. This policy intelligently soft-partitions CPUs to prioritize latency-critical ad delivery tasks, dynamically adjusting resources and enhancing cache locality. The initial deployment resulted in a 28% reduction in ads retrieval p99 latency, 3.28 megawatts of power savings, and a 1.1% increase in ads ranked. Subsequent user-space updates further improved performance, establishing a rapid and continuous optimization platform for their critical ad services.
