How and Why Netflix Built a Real-Time Distributed Graph: Part 3 — Querying the graph with gRPC… ↗

Netflix details the design of the serving layer for its Real-Time Distributed Graph (RDG), built to query billions of nodes and edges with sub-100ms latency using gRPC. To manage diverse workloads ranging from shallow, high-fan-out queries to deep, multi-hop traversals, the query engine employs a breadth-first execution strategy powered by async-first, dedicated thread pools and adaptive concurrency control. The engine streams and filters adjacency lists directly at the source, applies hierarchical filters like temporal windows, and selectively caches stable node metadata using EVCache. These optimizations allow Netflix to process tens of thousands of complex, highly concurrent graph queries per second while maintaining high throughput and low tail latency.

GenRec: Towards LLM-Native Recommendation at Netflix ↗

Netflix introduced GenRec, an LLM-native recommendation ranking system that replaces traditional feature-engineered models by converting user histories, item metadata, and context into natural-language prompts. The system employs a two-phase training framework that adapts a foundation LLM to Netflix data before fine-tuning it with a catalog-aware scoring head and reward-weighted loss functions to balance business constraints and long-term member utility. To serve the model efficiently at scale, GenRec applies context engineering to compact interaction histories and executes on vLLM in a prefill-only mode, scoring entire candidate sets in a single forward pass without autoregressive decoding. In online A/B testing covering 10% of Netflix traffic, GenRec achieved statistically significant gains over the production baseline in short-term and long-term metrics while utilizing up to 40 times fewer training examples.

Toward More Controllable AI Video Editing: An Early Research Exploration at Netflix ↗

Netflix researchers introduce two new generative AI video editing frameworks, Vera and VOID, designed to give artists precise control over edits while preserving the integrity of original footage. Vera uses a novel layered video diffusion model and a Mixture-of-Transformers architecture to isolate changes like object addition and background replacement into separate edit layers, preventing unintended alterations to the rest of the scene. VOID addresses video object and interaction deletion by integrating a Vision-Language Model reasoning pipeline and quadmask conditioning to simulate physically plausible counterfactual outcomes, such as recalculating gravity and collisions when an object is removed. Evaluations and human preference studies with creative reviewers demonstrate that both models significantly outperform existing baselines in content preservation and physical plausibility.

Thinking Fast & Slow for a Personalized Notification System ↗

Netflix redesigned its personalized notification system by introducing a hierarchical, slow-fast architecture that balances immediate user engagement with long-term retention. Instead of relying on a single causal model that optimizes solely for short-term outcomes and suffers from coupled frequency and ranking decisions, the new system separates strategy from execution. A "slow" policy operates on a weekly cadence to determine personalized message pacing and frequency across channels using a custom utility function, while a "fast" policy handles real-time message selection within those strategic guardrails. This decoupling prevents notification fatigue, safely manages opt-out risks, and delivered substantial metric lifts particularly among casual viewers.
